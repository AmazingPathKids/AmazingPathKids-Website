The gap

Most worry starts at the kitchen table, not in a clinic.

What usually follows is a late-night search that ends in a frightening list, or a well-meant “they all go at their own pace” with nothing to do in the meantime. UpPrism sits between the two. When a question belongs with a professional, it says so plainly.

How it works

Four steps. A clearer way forward.

Understand, prioritize, practise, track. A few minutes each time, then start the cycle again as your child grows.

  1. 1

    Understand

    Start with what you already see.

    A short check-in asks about everyday behaviour across eight areas. You answer Not yet, Sometimes or Consistently. There is nothing to test and no equipment to find. At the end you get a profile written in plain language.

  2. 2

    Prioritize

    Three priorities, suggested for you.

    Working on everything at once wears everyone out. Based on your check-in, UpPrism suggests three areas to start with and explains why. If one doesn’t fit, you can replace it with another area.

  3. 3

    Practise

    One activity, and the reason it helps.

    Each activity fits your child’s age and the areas you chose. It comes with a short note on why it helps, what you need from around the house, and steps you can follow with your child in front of you.

  4. 4

    Track

    Look back, then start the next turn.

    After each activity you can note how it went. Over weeks, your check-ins and notes become a history you can read at a glance, and bring to an appointment if you choose to.

Clear limits

UpPrism helps you notice and act. It does not diagnose.

UpPrism is

  • ✓A structured way to describe what you see your child doing.
  • ✓Home activities matched to your child’s age and your chosen focus, each with a reason.
  • ✓A history of check-ins and notes that you control.

UpPrism is not

  • A diagnosis, or a test that can rule anything in or out.
  • A screening tool for autism, ADHD or any other condition.
  • Medical advice, or a replacement for your doctor, nurse practitioner, speech-language pathologist or occupational therapist.

If you are worried about your child’s development, talk to your family doctor, pediatrician or nurse practitioner. In Ontario you can also call Health811 at 811 to speak with a registered nurse. In an emergency, call 911.
